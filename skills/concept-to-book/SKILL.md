# Concept to Book

Run a complete fiction workflow with visible reference provenance. This is a standalone skill: it requires no other skill, past chat, particular vault, publisher, author, or software package. Use the available file, research, image, and document tools. The bundled synthetic example illustrates the workflow; its choices are not defaults.

## Begin at the actual stage

Read the user's supplied concept or draft and existing project records. Determine the intended deliverable: plan, manuscript, assembled reading proof, publication files, or a live publication action. Recover accepted decisions before asking questions. Continue authorized work through that deliverable, with concise progress updates.

For an existing project, identify the current canonical files, latest completed checkpoint, unresolved issues, and next dependency. Do not restart its conception or replace an approved draft. Dates and labels such as “final” are insufficient: compare the actual register and output provenance. Historical snapshots stay historical.

For a new project, establish only the choices that affect the work: premise, audience, genre, book/series scope, original work versus adaptation, viewpoint, voice, content boundaries, length intent, and output location. Record provisional decisions. Do not impose this case study's particular book/chapter counts, cultural setting, or trim size.

Create or reuse these working records as they become necessary:

- Project brief and decision log.
- Canon/continuity ledger and source-use ledger.
- Series/book plan and chapter outlines.
- Chapter files and continuous reading copies.
- Revision register and unresolved-issue list.
- Assembly manifest and production status.

Keep records small enough to use. Do not duplicate the same authority across competing “current” files.

## Make vault use visible

Use [reference-provenance.md](references/reference-provenance.md) whenever consulting a vault, earlier manuscript, craft guide, source work, factual research, or another project's production code.

Before each stage, identify which references answer its actual questions. Search narrowly, read the relevant content, and log what changed because of it. Tell the user when a new reference materially informs a decision. Include exact paths and sections/pages, not merely an author or vault name.

Distinguish **consulted**, **applied**, **available but not consulted**, **unavailable**, and **historical use documented by a report**. Do not retroactively claim a guide influenced an earlier draft. Keep creative inspiration, story canon, factual support, and production reuse separate. A full text extraction or automated scan does not establish a complete close reading.

If no vault is provided, proceed from supplied material and record that no vault references were used. If a necessary source is missing, explain the specific gap and continue independent work; do not invent its contents, edition, quotations, or verification.

## 1. Develop the concept and story architecture

1. Write the story promise, protagonist's initial belief, conflict, stakes, and intended change in behavior.
2. For adaptations, map original episodes, roles, themes, and ending functions. Mark retained, relocated, combined, altered, and newly invented elements. Record the actual edition consulted.
3. Give characters practical lives, competing wants, skills, relationships, and consequences that survive the adventure. Modern occupations should affect decisions rather than merely rename a mythical identity.
4. Establish geography, chronology, rules of the world, institutional incentives, and travel constraints. In a historical or period setting, keep a private calendar and research period-specific material details.
5. When crossing cultures or traditions, retain local expertise and distinct beliefs. Separate documented tradition from fictional powers, institutions, agreements, and invented premises.
6. For a series, give every volume its own achievement and cost while developing the larger question. Build a revelation ladder: what each character and the reader may know, where it is seeded, and when it pays off. Check titles, maps, recaps, profiles, and promotional copy against that ladder.

Output: an actionable plan with character arcs, source/adaptation map where applicable, route/timeline, unresolved research questions, and a setup/payoff ledger, including cross-book dependencies for a series.

## 2. Outline at consistent depth

For each chapter, capture viewpoint, place/time, immediate objective, resistance, scene beats, consequential turn, exit condition, and narrative function. Tie it to the book arc and any source episode. Identify what must be researched before drafting.

Match planning depth across books, not a fixed number of bullets or chapters. A short outline must not quietly become the standard for later volumes. Check causality, transit, stakes, character agency, knowledge limits, and the intended ending before drafting at scale.

## 3. Draft and maintain continuity

Draft a representative opening, incorporate the user's direction, then continue in meaningful chapter or book units. Read the applicable outline, previous ending, continuity ledger, and needed sources before each unit.

Track injuries, possessions, promises, debts, relationships, names, abilities, travel time, elapsed time, knowledge, unresolved threats, and changes in belief. A later lesson should build on previous conduct rather than reset it. At each checkpoint, compile a continuous reading copy, update the ledger, record actual counts, and name the next unfinished section.

Preserve earlier versions before substantial revision. Separate narrative word counts from front matter, companion stories, notes, and reference text so comparisons remain meaningful.

## 4. Revise depth, voice, and development

Assess completed material before expanding it. Strengthen underdeveloped scenes with action, resistance, setting that affects choices, social friction, emotional consequence, and aftermath. Do not pad to make books equal in length.

When the user supplies craft influences, extract transferable principles into the source-use ledger. Apply them through concrete scene decisions and an original project voice. Record which scene changed and why. Do not assume all available guides were used.

Use separate passes when they answer different questions:

- Development: missing causal steps, local agency, difficult choices, earned change, aftermath, payoff.
- Voice: distinguish what each character notices and wants; improve humor, wonder, sensory specificity, and accessibility.
- Source/world alignment: verify original-episode functions, geography, chronology, traditions, supernatural limits, and adaptation inventions.
- Prose: remove repeated explanation, duplicated actions, bland abstractions, and any project-specific unwanted habits without flattening purposeful voice or period register.

Keep a revision register with issue, evidence, change, affected files, and follow-up check. Pilot a substantial treatment when that will clarify it; once direction is established, apply it consistently within the authorized scope. Before applying staged edits, compare their source hashes with the live files. Reconcile intervening changes instead of overwriting newer text with an old checkpoint.

## 5. Reassess chapter structure and series continuity

Split where a meaningful turn creates a better reading break. Merge when a break interrupts necessary momentum. Add a chapter only if it earns a new choice, complication, relationship, or consequence. Preserve the distinction between narrative development and arbitrary chapter-count parity.

After restructuring, maintain an old-to-new chapter map. Update outlines, indexes, continuity entries, recaps, companions, source notes, internal links, and assembly order. Audit pacing and the mystery's timing again.

Run cross-book checks on promises, property, injuries, debts, character knowledge, irreversible losses, capability limits, names, travel, and emotional endings. New powers or an earlier exceptional rescue must not silently become universal solutions. Treat user-approved canon changes as changes, not proof that the old version was always correct.

## 6. Add reader material only when it earns its place

Possible additions include author prefaces, journey recaps, companion stories, epilogue, folklore/adaptation afterwords, sources, author pages, character profiles, pronunciation guides, route maps, and illustrations. Select by the reader's need and the project's scope.

- Recaps may reveal only earlier books. Profiles and maps obey their placement's knowledge boundary.
- Companion stories need a distinct local or personal question, consequence, and placement rationale. They must not steal the main ending or manufacture length.
- Afterwords distinguish original source material, real cultural context, deliberate adaptation, and new invention.
- Sources preserve actual authorship, edition, dates, and access limits. Display links readably in the target medium; maintain one destination registry if links are reused.
- Author identity, biography, imprint, links, images, and publication claims must come from supplied or verified information.

Recheck plot, pace, reveals, chronology, and names after additions or global renames. Do not rely on an unreviewed global text replacement.

## 7. Complete a declared reading review

Distinguish a selective scan, a full consecutive read, a simulated reader response, independent reader feedback, and cultural review. Say which occurred. A checklist or word-count script cannot replace the claimed reading.

For a full review, read every included section in order, including front/back matter. Maintain a coverage record and repair log. Verify repairs in context and update affected canon records. Keep unresolved independent-feedback needs visible without claiming they happened.

## 8. Assemble reproducible reading proofs

Lock a source snapshot and manifest before building. Record source paths and hashes, exact section order, title/author/edition metadata, assets and placement rules, font origins, and output paths. Log any reused builder, fonts, configuration, or publishing records from the vault as production references.

Build the requested reading formats with a reproducible process. Keep reader text separate from author-facing plans and provenance logs. Clean internal wiki links and placeholders appropriately. If an existing production builder is unavailable, implement the needed assembly with tools present; this skill has no dependency on the case study's scripts or fonts.

Check text inclusion and order, section counts, typography, embedded fonts, missing glyphs, internal links, running headers/footers, page numbering, chapter openings, blank pages, and awkward line endings. Inspect rendered pages, especially repaired pages and atypical layouts. Typography problems should first be solved through layout; rewrite prose only when appropriate to the user's request and recheck meaning.

## 9. Integrate visual and navigation assets

Create maps, portraits, author images, illustrations, and cover concepts only when requested or clearly within scope. Base them on current canon and the reader's knowledge at placement. Keep stable character identifiers and appearance variants by time/book.

Use an asset manifest recording source/generated origin, version, intended scene, spoiler constraints, dimensions, placement, and verification. Place images according to narrative relevance and printable area; do not fill every gap mechanically. Check cropping, proportions, resolution, captions, and page flow, then regenerate and inspect affected proofs.

When covers are in scope, use the chronological sequence below with [cover-design-workflow.md](references/cover-design-workflow.md). Its CD identifiers connect each operation to its detailed checks; they are not twelve compulsory review rounds. Resume an approved design at its actual unfinished operation.

1. **Recover the current direction and inputs (CD01).** Identify the latest user choices, selected artwork/logo, copy, layout settings, interior, and edition records. Distinguish creative approval from local checks and live status; a Final folder is not proof of authority.
2. **Define consistent identity and permitted variation (CD02).** Establish logo/title/volume/author hierarchy and placement, then decide what may vary in scene, pose, palette, visible challenge, and cast. Apply later relaxed composition or selective-cast direction to its scope. Keep lettering independently controllable from scene artwork where useful.
3. **Bound edition geometry (CD03).** Identify front proportions, bindings, jacket panels, and the real inputs still needed for exact templates. Early art may use disclosed assumptions; do not invent final spine or flap dimensions.
4. **Brief and select story-specific art (CD04).** Consult relevant current chapters, appearance references, and approved portraits for focal cast, objects, action, and spoiler limits. Record actual source use, candidate selections, and delivered dimensions; requested pixel size does not establish received size.
5. **Test the assembled front when useful (CD05).** Combine selected art, logo, and actual typography before scaling. Inspect a difficult title/background or restrictive crop as relevant, protecting focal subjects and deliberate series variety. A front preview does not verify a full wrap or jacket.

## 10. Prepare publication files within the authorized scope

Reading proofs, upload-ready files, uploaded files, platform-processed files, approved proofs, and published/distributed editions are different states. Name the highest verified state for each edition.

Continue the cover sequence with the applicable edition inputs and the [portable cover workflow](references/cover-design-workflow.md):

6. **Write each copy surface for its purpose (CD06).** Define back-blurb versus inner-flap roles, biography and portrait treatment, URLs/capitalization, visible publishing brand, and spoiler boundaries. Preserve supplied identity facts. Visible brand text does not establish a provider-assigned imprint.
7. **Design spines and supporting panels (CD07).** When requested, choose story-grounded icons with chapter/appearance evidence. Balance apparent size and stroke weight at actual spine scale; equal bounding boxes need not look equal. Coordinate title/author/icon, portrait, flaps, and back branding.
8. **Fit type and contrast (CD08).** Check the actual fonts, sizes, widths, backgrounds, line endings, and justified spacing. Repair layers or layout while preserving approved art; if wording changes are authorized, revise semantically at the agreed size and retain facts/voice/spoiler limits. Do not silently shrink type or impose gold, outlines, borders, or new numeric fit targets.
9. **Assemble each edition independently (CD09).** Obtain current specifications and the exact applicable template for title, author, imprint, trim, binding, paper, ISBN, and verified interior page count. Finalize local geometry from real available inputs; later reconcile any platform-processed/manufacturing count before claiming acceptance. Local file preparation need not await processing that has not occurred. Distinguish trim/bleed/spine/folds/flaps/hinges, full submission sheet, review overlay, and cropped preview; preserve the correct barcode and required template content. Do not stretch a paperback wrap into a jacket.
10. **Inspect the rendered set (CD10).** Check fronts, backs, spines, flaps, folds, copy, objects, cropping, alignment, portrait treatment, and barcode clearance at reading/print scale. Use a contact sheet for series consistency and detailed views for individual defects. Consolidate repairs and inspect affected neighboring areas.
11. **Diagnose and repair technical defects (CD11).** Measure actual pixels and effective resolution at placed size, including small icons and masks, plus applicable font/color/ink/transparency/format requirements. Distinguish excessive resolution from insufficient detail. Preserve approved inputs and repair the offending object; DPI labels alone add no detail. Follow the shared workflow's source/derivative and restoration rules, available-tool instructions, and structural-plus-rendered verification. Do not claim formal conformance or printer acceptance from a local checklist.
12. **Verify the exact file pair and handoff (CD12).** Record current cover/interior hashes and any transport derivative for the correct edition and assigned metadata. For authorized uploads, distinguish transfer progress, dated receipt, file-pair validation, submission, processing, proof approval, and distribution. After interruption or manual changes, reconcile saved state before retrying. A validated cover or catalog status alone does not identify the intended interior.

Report unresolved metadata, manufacturing, template, and file-version issues precisely. If interior count or other geometry inputs change, recheck the affected covers. Live uploads, purchases, final approvals, and distribution follow the user's actual authorization; completing a manuscript alone supplies no such authority.

## Finish with evidence

Deliver the requested files with a concise account of what changed, what was checked, remaining issues, and exact next status. Include a human-readable list of references actually used and their effects. A resumable checkpoint names the authoritative files and the next concrete action.
