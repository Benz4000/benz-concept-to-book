# Synthetic concept-to-book example

This public example uses invented, generic material. Private case studies, manuscript details, conversations, source-vault locations and publication records are omitted. It does not document a real person's project or prove that any source was read.

An author wants a fantasy novel about a traveller learning to keep promises. The goal is a complete local reading proof. The story needs an earned change in conduct, independent supporting characters and an ending that resolves its main conflict.

1. Recover the requested endpoint and existing accepted work.
2. Establish audience, story promise, adaptation boundaries and voice.
3. Map character goals, causality, chronology, knowledge and reveals.
4. Consult relevant available sources and record the actual scope read.
5. Outline chapters with objective, resistance, turn and consequence.
6. Test representative prose or design when uncertainty warrants it.
7. Draft the planned narrative and supporting material.
8. Repair demonstrated structural and character gaps.
9. Polish prose while preserving protected qualities.
10. Complete intended recaps, profiles, citations and navigation.
11. Read the integrated edition and record actual coverage.
12. Assemble requested outputs from an exact source snapshot.
13. Inspect actual pages, assets, links and relevant reader behaviour.
14. Apply the complete cover workflow when covers are requested, then match each cover to its current interior.
15. Hand off exact files with actual evidence and pending checks; retain a recoverable previous release.

For example, a craft note about ordinary wants might lead to a scene where the traveller's promise conflicts with a local person's livelihood. Record the note/excerpt actually read, the interpretation and the affected scene. This illustrative application is not a source quotation or a historical reading claim.

Use the installed version's actual capabilities. This example does not add later helpers to an earlier version or require a particular chapter count, format, publisher, cultural setting or author influence. See [the skill](../SKILL.md) and [cover workflow](cover-design-workflow.md).
