# Observable quality and useful work order

Use this reference when subjective goals need a usable acceptance check, issues compete for attention, or repeated work can share inputs. Extend the current Foundation and issue register; do not create a separate scoring system.

## Turn the intended experience into evidence

For each consequential criterion, record the goal, scope, requirement/target/preference status, observable result, available benchmark, protected qualities, review method, and actual coverage. Use accepted examples when available. Otherwise label examples proposed or illustrative; never invent user acceptance.

The following are adaptable examples, not requirements for every story:

| Quality | Observable evidence to inspect | Example gap | Preserve during repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scene depth and agency | A character's want meets meaningful resistance; a choice affects the outcome | The protagonist receives a solution without a consequential choice | Intentional quiet scenes, indirect action, varied scene length |
| Causality and payoff | The outline or prose connects a setup, decision, consequence, and intended payoff | The climax depends on an ability or decision with no support | Deliberate surprise and legitimately open threads |
| Voice and explanation | Relevant passages match the scoped voice examples; readers can follow what they need without repeated explanation | Narration repeats the same inference after action and dialogue already show it | Local register, humor, ambiguity, and necessary orientation |
| Source and adaptation clarity | A claim maps to the actual consulted source; invented departures are distinguishable | A secondary note is presented as a verified traditional account | The project's permitted invention and original voice |
| Reader material | Each included section serves its stated purpose at its placement and respects its knowledge boundary | A front-of-book profile reveals an intended late discovery | Useful orientation and curiosity |
| Visual and layout fit | The actual rendered sample/output meets chosen legibility, crop, continuity, and edition criteria | A protected face is cropped or a heading overlaps body text | Approved composition, text, and intentional whitespace |
| Handoff accuracy | Delivered files match the recorded versions and the claimed state has direct evidence | “Approved” is inferred from an upload receipt | Distinct local, platform, and proof-review states |

Choose evidence appropriate to the endpoint: an outline can demonstrate planned causality but cannot prove finished prose rhythm or rendered page quality. Name the inspected unit and input/output version. A scan, selective reading, full consecutive reading, simulation, actual reader report, and rendered inspection answer different questions.

State the result as a finding with evidence: met, gap, uncertain, or not applicable, with the project's equivalent check status. Do not collapse literary quality into an invented universal numerical score or average away a blocking defect. Measured counts, dimensions, or user-selected thresholds are useful when relevant; label their basis and limits. A benchmark is a comparison aid, not permission to copy wording or make every scene alike.

## Distinguish blockers from optional improvements

A finding blocks the affected commitment when it violates an applicable requirement, leaves required review coverage absent, or makes a necessary input unreliable. Identify the commitment precisely: sustained drafting, a specific scene, final cover geometry, release claim, or another dependency. Missing final spine width blocks the final wrap; it does not block an independent chapter.

A target or preference can motivate a worthwhile improvement without becoming a new completion gate. An optional scene, extra image, stricter whitespace target, or unrequested review is not automatically required. A serious observed defect may justify broader repair, but explain the goal and evidence rather than silently expanding scope.

For an accepted exception, cite the actual authority or existing rule allowing it and its scope. Do not invent acceptance, convert a failed requirement into a pass, or describe deferred blocking work as complete. When evidence is missing, record uncertainty and obtain it before the claim it supports. Ask only when an unresolved choice materially changes authorized work; continue work independent of that choice.

## Order work by consequences and dependencies

Use judgment over the dependency map, not a fabricated priority formula:

1. Identify the current endpoint and user priorities. Resolve issues that prevent that endpoint or would make substantial dependent work unreliable.
2. Prefer an upstream decision or repair when it governs many imminent outputs, invalidates expensive review, or unlocks several ready tasks. Inspect likely dependencies when the map is incomplete; missing links are not evidence of no impact.
3. Group tasks sharing authoritative inputs, a common correction, and compatible review scope. For example, propagate a confirmed character-name decision through prose, recap, and metadata in one batch, then perform each relevant semantic and output check.
4. Keep evidence types distinct. A batch search does not replace reading changed scenes in context; checking page geometry does not establish narrative continuity.
5. Run independent ready work while a specific dependency waits. Record safe deferrals with the missing input, milestone, blocked actions, and what may continue. Avoid bulk work on a premise likely to be overturned.
6. After a consequential change, update affected outputs and review status; retain checks whose relevant inputs are demonstrably unchanged. Stop when the requested endpoint and its required checks are met.

A useful queue entry is: `issue/goal -> affected work -> blocking dependency -> reason for order -> shared batch -> completion evidence -> invalidated checks`. Add an owner only when coordination needs one. Do not estimate numerical impact, effort, or certainty without a basis.

Illustrative ordering: a changed ending may come before dependent recaps and ending artwork; an unavailable printer template may wait while prose proceeds; a cosmetic cover refinement may follow a broken navigation repair for a reading-proof endpoint. Actual user priorities can change this order. Reevaluate on new direction, a changed dependency, or a failed check, not on every minor edit.
