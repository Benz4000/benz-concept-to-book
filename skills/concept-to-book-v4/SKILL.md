# Concept to Book — Version 4

Establish the decisions that otherwise cause late rewrites or redesigns before their dependent work scales. Draft and produce to that foundation, then use later stages to revise and polish the planned book. Retain the ability to change direction when the user requests it or the evidence warrants it.

This skill is standalone. It requires no earlier-version installation, prior chat, private vault, other skill, publisher, or specific software. The bundled public example is synthetic, not a required input or a set of defaults. Version 4 retains foundation-first planning and outcome-based completion, adds preference scope/examples, connected pilots, drift/preservation checks, explicit decision dependencies, and rework learning. Its separate skill-evaluation suite tests transfer to different projects. Preserve `concept-to-book`, `concept-to-book-v2`, and `concept-to-book-v3`; all operational guidance needed here is bundled in this folder.

## Start with the user's end goals

Read available user decisions, project files, and current outputs. Determine the requested endpoint: concept/outline, manuscript, illustrated reading proof, publication files, or authorized platform action. Resume at the actual stage and stop at that endpoint. Do not infer publication authority from a request to write or format a book.

Create a compact goal register. For each goal record:

| Goal | Evidence and priority | Observable completion | Current gap | Owning stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What the user wants readers or the deliverable to experience | Explicit request or labeled inference; requirement, target, or preference | Concrete evidence of the intended result | Unmet, met, uncertain, or not applicable | A–L below |

Distinguish the requested end from a proposed tactic. “Make the books feel equally developed” may require better scenes, not identical word counts. “Reduce wasted page space” may require layout repair, not another illustration. A particular number becomes a constraint only when the user makes it one.

Recover conflicting or superseded decisions. Apply the latest explicit choice to the affected goal. Ask only when the remaining ambiguity materially changes the work; otherwise state a reasonable assumption and progress. Stage completion is an evidence check, not a new permission gate.

For a new project or workflow migration, read [foundation-settings.md](references/foundation-settings.md) for the decision register and readiness checks. Read the relevant parts of [calibration-and-learning.md](references/calibration-and-learning.md) when establishing preference scope, running a pilot, checking drift, tracing a consequential change, or learning from rework. Keep these records with the project, merging them into existing records. The optional [historical goal audit](references/goal-audit-example.md) maps 58 past operations to outcomes; it is not required reading for a new book.

The [public cover example](references/cover-workflow-example.md) is synthetic. Apply the complete CD01–CD12 sequence at the relevant dependencies; private conversations and source-specific values are omitted.

## Establish the foundation before scaling

Create or reconcile one **Book Foundation** with the goal register, accepted settings, source-use log, and intended reader-section inventory. Recover existing decisions first. For an ongoing project, fill missing settings around the current work; do not restart an accepted manuscript merely to create a new planning document.

Capture the following before the work they govern. Use a known decision, a clearly labeled reasonable assumption, or an explicit deferred item with a resolution milestone. Do not fill missing preferences with the example project's exact choices.

| Foundation area | Set early | Resolve before |
|---|---|---|
| Story and scope | Audience, premise, source/adaptation treatment, series/book scope, character arcs, setting/time, cultural boundaries, reveal strategy, ending intent | Detailed outlines and sustained drafting |
| Craft and depth | Available author-study principles, original voice, viewpoint, humor/wonder, readability, explanation level, punctuation preferences, benchmark scene depth | Bulk drafting; sample first when direction is uncertain |
| Structure and balance | Intended chapter experience/count flexibility, main/side-story roles, volume-balance measure, length targets versus quality priorities | Chapter architecture and planned content budgets |
| Complete reader package | Per-book prefaces, recaps, companions, epilogue, lore/afterwords, citations, author pages, guides, profiles, pronunciation, maps; purpose/order/spoiler boundary | Main drafting for narrative-dependent additions; final writing can follow stable canon |
| Identity and sources | Stable character/book IDs, working names/titles and alias policy, source edition, source-use tracking, citation/link presentation, supplied author text/photos | Repeated naming/citation work; final metadata before release |
| Visual plan | Art style, coverage roles/density, character continuity, image shapes, map/profile treatment, spoiler rules, cover family direction | Large image batches; scene briefs wait for sufficiently stable scenes |
| Edition and layout | Intended formats, trim/binding where relevant, typography, margins, furniture, blank/chapter-start policy, image placement, whitespace/balance preferences, preservation priorities | Representative proof and bulk assembly/art production |
| Completion and dependencies | Requested stopping state, required review coverage, exceptions, unresolved facts, owner and milestone for each deferred item | Any dependent commitment; actual final counts/templates remain late facts |

**Separate preferences from computed facts.** Decide the visual/edition strategy early; do not invent final page counts, assigned ISBNs, exact spine widths, printer processing, or scene-specific research that is not yet available. Mark deferred items as unknown with the stage that needs them. A deferred cover dimension must not block writing a chapter.

**Calibrate preferences within their scope.** Record author, series, book, or edition scope, explicit direction versus inference, accepted/unwanted examples, and exceptions when useful. Current explicit direction governs its scope; a local exception does not rewrite wider preferences. An inferred preference or a vault recommendation is a candidate, not a user instruction. Reuse established author preferences when relevant, without silently promoting project choices into future defaults.

**Test how choices work together.** When significant uncertainty warrants a pilot, carry one representative unit through the same applicable chain: outline, prose, revision, supporting material, art, assembly, and rendered check, stopping at the requested endpoint. Choose a unit exposing consequential uncertainties and reuse accepted work. Record which links actually ran, what the evidence establishes, and what remains untested; a plan or placeholder does not prove integration. Add another sample only for a material risk the first cannot cover. Outline-only work ends with outline validation; do not manufacture prose, art, future canon, ISBNs, or final production facts for the pilot.

**Choose priorities before repeated repair.** Distinguish required settings from targets/preferences. Resolve foreseeable conflicts such as zero blanks versus recto-only chapter starts, exact page parity versus meaningful content, or image coverage versus page/quality limits. Use the user's expressed priorities; ask about a real unresolved tradeoff with a concrete example rather than administering a long intake questionnaire.

**Readiness:** dependent work may scale when its necessary settings are established, relevant conflicts are resolved, and open items have a safe, specific deferral. Record the working foundation version and the evidence supporting it. This is an internal readiness check, not a new approval requirement. New user direction can revise it.

## Execute the plan, then revise and polish

Use this sequence while retaining the stage letters below for ownership and change tracking:

1. **Foundation and calibration:** A plus the settings for E, F, H, J, and L. Recover scoped preferences and examples, determine relevant guidance, and use a connected pilot when uncertainty warrants it before scaling dependent work.
2. **Planned creation:** B/C and the commissioned supporting material. Draft against the foundation; keep sources, canon, and the reader inventory current. Planned companions and arcs are part of this phase, not default late additions. At meaningful milestones, check representative new work for drift against the current foundation; link consequential decisions to their implementations.
3. **Editorial revision:** D/E/F/G. Repair development, structure, prose, reader material, and continuity against the intended experience. Use an evidenced gap or new direction to justify substantial additions. State what each repair must preserve and compare before/after evidence as well as the intended improvement.
4. **Production:** verify H, then execute I/J using established specifications and sufficiently stable content. Finalize actual text/assets and assemble them; avoid reopening style choices without a reason.
5. **Polish and handoff:** K/L. Resolve rendered-page and cover defects, verify exact edition inputs, package the requested deliverables, report their evidenced state, and capture a few supported lessons from substantial rework for the next project.

Later review should normally improve execution of the agreed plan. It must not automatically commission more stories, mythology, profiles, illustrations, or another design merely because those options exist. Necessary restructuring remains allowed: identify the defect or new goal, update the foundation and dependent records, and scope the rework. Reducing avoidable overhaul is a process aim, not a promise that creative discovery will require no changes.

For covers, scope accepted and proposed examples by series/book/edition; connect a demanding title, restrictive crop, and narrow copy surface in one relevant pilot. Trace user/source decisions through scenes, icons, copy, layout config, edition templates, output hashes, and handoff evidence. Apply the cover-specific calibration and rework examples in [calibration-and-learning.md](references/calibration-and-learning.md); a repaired image or changed color does not automatically invalidate unrelated prose or layout evidence.

## Check drift and preserve successful qualities

Choose checkpoints based on scale, changed inputs, a new scene type, recurring feedback, or other evidence of risk. Do not impose a review on every chapter. Compare a small relevant sample with accepted examples and the current foundation for voice, depth, explanation, character agency, source treatment, or visual coverage. Record sample/criterion, finding, action, and reading scope in the existing issue record. Intentional variation and accepted new direction are not drift. Sampling does not replace a required full reading or rendered acceptance.

Before repairing an issue, identify protected qualities such as emotional effect, deliberate ambiguity, local register, established facts, or a reveal boundary. Check both the intended improvement and those qualities afterward. If a requested change supersedes a protected quality, record the tradeoff and follow that direction. Expand the repair only when evidence warrants it. See [calibration and learning](references/calibration-and-learning.md) for compact examples.

## Audit before proposing another pass

Compare current artifacts with the goal register and Book Foundation, then log concrete gaps. Every proposed task must have:

- An end goal and identifiable defect, unmet requirement, or new user request.
- A named artifact or reading scope to inspect/change.
- The reference needed, if any, and a reason to read it.
- A completion check and the outputs that the change may invalidate.

Combine tasks that share an outcome, authoritative inputs, and review scope. Separate work when its evidence differs: a structural scan, full consecutive reading, simulated reader response, independent beta/cultural review, and rendered-page inspection cannot substitute for one another.

Keep one active issue register per project. Do not create another undifferentiated “final final pass” when the existing list and review scope can answer the question. If the user explicitly requests a fresh comprehensive review, perform it and state its coverage. Otherwise, a completed check with unchanged relevant inputs remains valid.

## Apply the relevant outcome checks

Use [outcome-stages.md](references/outcome-stages.md) for each stage's end goal, work, completion evidence, and reopen conditions. It contains the full standalone workflow:

| Stage | Outcome |
|---|---|
| A | Clear creative contract and usable foundation |
| B | Actionable, sufficiently researched architecture |
| C | Complete draft at consistent depth |
| D | Developmental revision that earns the story's consequences and ending |
| E | Voice/prose polish that preserves intended qualities |
| F | Complete and consistent reader package |
| G | Editorially coherent reading edition |
| H | Verified edition specification |
| I | Reproducible assembled manuscript |
| J | Coherent visual edition, when requested |
| K | Verified rendered edition, when requested |
| L | Matching covers and accurate production handoff, when requested |

These are responsibility groups, not twelve mandatory passes. Establish relevant E/F/H/J/L preferences in the foundation, execute them at the appropriate later stage, and stop at the requested endpoint. A concept or outline normally ends at A or B.

## Manage changes by their effects

Use the change-impact table in [outcome-stages.md](references/outcome-stages.md). Classify a change as a repair within the foundation, a refinement within existing choices, a deferred fact becoming known, or a new creative/design requirement. Update the foundation when a setting changes and identify which completed evidence no longer holds. Honor authorized new direction; do not treat the foundation as irrevocable or require approval already supplied by the user's request. Reuse unchanged sources/assets and completed checks. Record changed inputs, invalidated checks, scoped work, and restored status.

For consequential choices, connect a stable source/decision ID to the interpretation, scoped setting, affected chapter/section/asset/build input, and version-specific verification evidence. Keep links in existing records; an unsourced creative choice can have a decision ID with no source. When an input changes, follow those links, check whether the map covers the change, and inspect plausible omissions before claiming propagation is complete. Missing dependency records mean uncertainty, not proof that nothing is affected. Preserve valid unrelated checks.

A new companion story can reopen D/E/F/G/I/J/K and pagination-dependent L. A source URL repair may need F/I/K link verification while leaving plot review valid. An image resize can need J/I/K and page-count-dependent L without a new literary revision. Match scope to actual effects; do not assume every tool change alters every book.

## Preserve reference transparency

Use [reference-provenance.md](references/reference-provenance.md) whenever consulting sources. Record exact source, stage/goal, location read, reading scope, actual application, destination, and limitations. Separate supplied literature, imported craft notes, internal canon, factual research, user materials, and production reuse.

Tell the user when a new vault reference materially affects work. Distinguish consulted/applied from merely available, unavailable, or historical use documented by a report. Do not claim fresh source consultation simply because established principles continue to guide revision. If there is no vault, proceed with supplied material and say no vault references were used.

## Learn from substantial rework

When substantial rework happens, record its cause and evidence in the issue/change record: missing or misunderstood preference, changed direction, weak pilot/sample, factual correction, continuity failure, or valuable creative discovery. Causes can be mixed or uncertain. Note what an earlier decision or test could realistically have prevented; do not rewrite history or treat later unavailable information as an early failure.

At handoff, extract a few actionable lessons with their scope, evidence, next verification opportunity, and adoption status. Reuse explicit applicable preferences; keep inferred lessons as candidates until supported by user direction. Store project findings with the project; do not silently rewrite global preferences or this reusable skill. Fewer revisions alone is not success: distinguish preventable rework from improvements worth making.

## Deliver the outcome and audit trail

Report goal status, completed outputs, review evidence, remaining issues, references actually used, a few useful lessons when supported, and the next concrete dependency if work remains. Stop when the requested deliverable is achieved; do not manufacture more improvement passes to keep working.

## Evaluate the skill when maintaining it

The sixth enhancement belongs to skill development, not every book. After consequential skill changes, use [skill-evaluation.md](references/skill-evaluation.md) and its bundled synthetic scenarios to evaluate actual behavior across different scopes. Inspect outputs for preference boundaries, connected-pilot honesty, preservation, source/decision traceability, rework learning, correct deferrals, and scope control. Distinguish passed, failed, blocked, and unrun cases; rerun only materially affected scenarios after repairs. Do not claim a behavioral test from frontmatter validation alone.
