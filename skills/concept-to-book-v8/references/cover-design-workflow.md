# Cover design and production workflow

Use this when covers, jackets, or their production files are in scope. It is self-contained and does not require the example project, another skill, or a specific tool or publisher. The companion [dialogue addendum](cover-workflow-example.md) provides a synthetic illustration; its choices and numerical checks are examples, not universal requirements.

The steps below are dependency groups. An existing approved design can enter at a targeted repair; a concept request stops before production. Combine related work and reuse checks whose relevant inputs remain unchanged.

In the A–L workflow, establish identity and edition strategy early (CD02–CD03), then let imagery, copy, and type/crop pilots interleave as their inputs become available (CD04–CD08). A pilot can test a front without waiting for final wrap geometry; use actual approved copy for fit claims. Stage I assembles the interior/reader edition, including a digital front cover when requested. Physical edition-specific wrap/jacket assembly is CD09 at L and requires the applicable template and page-count inputs. Review and repair that export (CD10–CD11, K) before exact-pair handoff (CD12, L). A front preview alone never validates a complete wrap or jacket.

| ID / step | Work and intended result | Completion evidence / reopen trigger |
|---|---|---|
| CD01 — Recover direction and authoritative inputs | Identify the requested endpoint, latest explicit choices, selected artwork, logo, copy, layout configuration, interior, edition record, and output manifest. Separate creative approval, local technical checks, and platform status. A folder called Final or an old summary does not establish current authority. | A versioned input/output map identifies the current design and each edition's actual print files. Reopen changed inputs only; investigate stale or conflicting records. |
| CD02 — Establish identity and permitted variation | Define series logo, title/volume/author hierarchy and placement; decide what remains consistent and what may vary by book. Set cast-selection, viewpoint, action/challenge, palette, and spoiler rules. Keep scene art, reusable logo, and typeset text independently controllable when useful. | A brief distinguishes fixed identity from creative freedom, with accepted examples or clearly labeled proposals. A later change in direction supersedes only affected earlier choices. |
| CD03 — Establish edition geometry and deferred facts | Record intended front proportions, paperback/hardcover/jacket variants, paper/binding, and reliable edition identity. Obtain the applicable current template when actual inputs exist. Distinguish front trim, bleed/artwork extent, spine, folds/flaps/hinges, safe areas, full submission sheet, and preview crop. | Each edition maps to its own template, ISBN where applicable, and page-count basis. Preliminary art can proceed with bounded assumptions; exact wraps wait for the relevant real inputs. Reconcile later processed/manufacturing counts before claiming acceptance. |
| CD04 — Brief story-specific imagery | Consult the actual chapters, appearance references, existing approved portraits, and relevant source material. Choose a scene, focal cast, pose/viewpoint, objects, visible challenge, and knowledge ceiling. Selective casts can improve composition when permitted; variety does not excuse canon errors. Record sources, prompts, selected/rejected candidates, and delivered dimensions. | Brief and selected art agree with canon and spoiler limits. A request for large pixels is not evidence of receiving them. Reopen for changed canon, appearance, composition direction, or deficient assets. |
| CD05 — Test the connected front design | When uncertainty warrants it, test selected art with the actual logo and typography before a batch. Include a difficult title, bright/busy background, important face/object, and restrictive edition crop as relevant. Inspect both individual covers and the intended series system. | The tested front retains legibility, focal subjects, and deliberate variety across applicable crops. A front preview alone does not validate a complete wrap or jacket. Add another example only for an important untested condition. |
| CD06 — Write each copy surface for its purpose | Specify the relationship between back blurb and inner-flap synopsis; avoid accidental duplication when distinct roles are intended. Set spoiler limits, portrait/biography treatment, author/site capitalization, URLs, and publishing-brand placement. Preserve supplied identity facts. Distinguish visible brand text from a provider-assigned bibliographic imprint. | Required copy surfaces are complete, accurate, purposefully related, and traceable to source text/user direction. Reopen affected copy for new canon, name, identity, inventory, or fit changes. |
| CD07 — Design the spine and supporting panels | If requested, choose a story-grounded artifact/icon per book, cite its chapter/appearance evidence, and separate decorative interpretation from canon. Balance apparent size and stroke weight at actual spine dimensions rather than forcing identical bounding boxes. Coordinate spine title/author/icon, portraits, flaps, and back branding. | Objects are recognizable, correctly scoped, spoiler-conscious, and optically coherent as a set. Actual-size proofs expose overly subtle or dominant icons. Portrait borders, gold, and decorative textures are preferences, not defaults. |
| CD08 — Fit typography and contrast | Use actual fonts, sizes, widths, and backgrounds. Check title fit, line endings, narrow justified columns, word spacing, and contrast. Prefer controlled layer/layout repairs for contrast; avoid flattening approved artwork unnecessarily. If wording changes are authorized, revise copy semantically at the agreed type size and preserve facts, meaning, voice, and spoiler limits. | Before/after evidence shows the intended improvement and preserved qualities in every affected surface/format. Do not silently shrink type, force breaks, pad copy, distort art, or apply a new numeric spacing target. Inspect edges/fades for halos or banding. |
| CD09 — Assemble edition-specific files | Build each wrap/jacket against its applicable template; do not stretch a paperback wrap into a jacket. Keep correct edition barcode/ISBN, page geometry, folds, safe areas, and the template elements required by the destination's instructions. Distinguish review overlays and cropped artwork previews from submission files. | Output manifest names exact sources, template, barcode, dimensions, and export settings. Required content appears once and fits its intended panel. Exclude proof-only guides unless the destination explicitly requires them. |
| CD10 — Review rendered design and copy | Inspect actual exported fronts, backs, spines, flaps, and fold/trim boundaries. Review at reading/print scale and in a series contact sheet for hierarchy and consistency. Verify text, objects, cropping, alignment, portrait treatment, and barcode clearance. Use one issue list and inspect neighboring areas after repairs. | Review scope and exact output version are recorded; fixes meet the established criteria without degrading protected qualities. A contact sheet or automated bounding-box test does not replace detailed inspection. |
| CD11 — Diagnose technical defects and repair the affected objects | Check delivered pixels and effective resolution at the placed physical size, including small icons and masks, plus applicable fonts, color/output profile, ink limits, transparency, barcode, and format rules. Use current destination requirements; diagnose whether a warning is too-low resolution, excessive resolution, or another issue. Preserve approved layers and target the offending object. | Verify the repair's actual pixels, placement, text/content, other assets, geometry, and relevant rendered differences. A DPI label alone adds no detail. Follow the restoration/provenance rules below; local checks do not establish printer acceptance or formal PDF/X conformity without the relevant validation. |
| CD12 — Verify the exact file pair and handoff state | Match each cover to its intended edition and current interior, including assigned ISBN/copyright-page updates when required. Track canonical and transport derivatives by hash. For authorized uploads, distinguish transfer progress from dated receipt, file-pair validation, submission, processing, proof approval, and distribution. After interruption or concurrent changes, inspect saved state before retrying. | Report the highest evidenced state per edition, outstanding warnings, and exact resubmission dependencies. Cover-only validation does not certify the intended interior. A catalog status does not identify file versions. No new commercial or live-action authority follows from this design workflow. |

## Resolution repairs and preservation

Compute effective resolution from actual image pixels and the placed physical dimensions, considering the actual crop/transform. Inspect every relevant embedded object; the full-page image and a tiny spine icon can have opposite problems. Preserve the unmodified source and record the selected derivative.

- For excessive embedded resolution, resample only the offending image/mask when appropriate. Preserve its physical placement and other artwork/text. Verify the exported result rather than assuming unchanged placement from an unchanged filename.
- For insufficient source pixels, inspect available better sources and actual returned generation dimensions. Do not repeat a proven ineffective method indefinitely or claim a requested size was delivered.
- Where restoration/upscaling is permitted by the user's direction and available tools, retain the approved composition and record the source, method, output dimensions, and selected print derivative. Label reconstructed detail honestly; it is not a native higher-resolution original. Inspect faces, hands, fine objects, lettering, and texture for altered meaning or artifacts. Do not treat upscaling as automatically acceptable or automatically forbidden.
- Use actual available tools under their governing instructions. A historical use of a particular generator, upscaler, builder, or PDF library does not require that tool in a future project or override image-editing rules.
- When feasible, compare protected content streams, text, placement matrices, page boxes, unrelated image/mask hashes, fonts, and output intent before/after a targeted repair. Pair structural checks with rendered inspection; byte preservation alone does not prove visual quality.

## Scope changes and completion

| Change | Reconsider | Usually retain when verified unchanged |
|---|---|---|
| More varied composition or selective cast | Scene brief, source/spoiler checks, art, crop, and front design | Established typography identity if still compatible |
| Heading color, logo contrast, or type edging | Affected layers, readability, neighboring subjects, color/export, and relevant renders | Copy wording/line breaks, geometry, approved source art |
| Back/flap rewording | Meaning, identity facts, spoilers, actual-font fit, and affected renders | Unrelated narrative/art review |
| Icon weight/size or portrait-border treatment | Canon/identity, actual-size appearance, placement, resolution, and affected output | Unrelated front art and copy |
| Resolution-only image replacement | Asset provenance, altered visual details, actual placed resolution, technical export, affected renders | Layout/text/barcodes only when comparisons demonstrate preservation |
| New interior or manufacturing count | Matching edition record, cover geometry, page-dependent template checks, exact file-pair validation | Unaffected edition designs and creative decisions |
| Session interruption or manual submission | Saved file identity, receipt, validation scope, state, and resubmission queue | Persisted files and earlier authorization supported by records |

Keep project-specific values out of universal rules: a 6×9 front, gold text, justified flaps, fixed two-line titles, parchment portraits, exact font sizes, word-spacing thresholds, particular icon objects, publisher names, and a platform's historical PPI limits all require their own scope/evidence.

Record references where they enter: user direction for design preferences; current chapters and appearance records for narrative imagery/icons; supplied biographies/photos for identity; template/edition records for geometry/barcodes; manifests and actual files for technical status. An older report is evidence of its checkpoint, not a fresh inspection of its cited source or a current live status.
