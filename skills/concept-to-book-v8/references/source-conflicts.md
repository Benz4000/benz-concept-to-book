# Conflicting sources and adaptation decisions

Use this reference when materially different accounts, editions, translations, interpretations or traditions affect the work. A conflict needs a scoped decision, not an invented consensus or an exhaustive survey. Follow [reference provenance](reference-provenance.md) for source identity and actual use; merge the [decision template](../assets/templates/source-decisions.md) into the existing source ledger or foundation.

## Identify what actually differs

1. Name the concrete question and affected destination: an event, relationship, name, custom, translated term, belief, chronology, image or reader-facing claim. Check whether the accounts address the same place, period, community and kind of claim.
2. Record each source's exact identity as known: title, creator, edition/translation, date, path or URL, and the actual passage or excerpt consulted. Record relevant missing metadata without inventing it. Reading a synopsis or a translator's note establishes that reading scope, not consultation of the whole source work.
3. Separate what the source says from its author's interpretation, your interpretation, the project's canon and proposed invention. A fictional narrator's assertion need not represent the source author's or a community's belief.
4. Classify the difference when supported: edition/translation variation, distinct tradition, interpretation, factual disagreement, project contradiction, or unresolved difference. Different accounts may coexist without one being erroneous. Do not label an unread source as agreeing or disagreeing.

Research only what can change the decision or its disclosure. An outline may proceed with a bounded assumption if it does not harden an unsupported claim into fact. If a decisive passage is unavailable, mark that limit and identify the work it blocks; continue independent work.

## Assess evidence for this question

There is no universal cultural-source ranking. Assess fitness for the actual claim: relevant community/place/period, the source's role and perspective, traceability to its evidence, independence, whether it addresses this question, and the scope actually inspected. An older text, newer study, first-person account, translation or official institution can serve different questions; none automatically represents all members of a tradition. A chosen edition can govern adaptation fidelity without settling historical or cultural truth.

Use current authoritative evidence when a consequential present-day factual claim or applicable rule requires it. Consult credentials, specialist commentary or additional sources when they help resolve the question; do not collect them as ritual fields. Record uncertainty and dissent that remain material. A model's familiarity or a search result snippet is not evidence of full-source reading. An independent reader's or specialist's response is evidence only if actually received, with its own scope; this workflow authorizes no contact.

Explicit user direction governs creative choices within its scope. It does not transform an unsupported factual assertion into verified fact. If an accepted story choice conflicts with research, distinguish the intended fictional departure from any reader-facing factual claim. Preserve the user's creative intent while correcting unsupported attribution.

## Choose and document the adaptation

For a consequential conflict, record:

| Field | Required distinction |
|---|---|
| Question and scope | Which story/edition/claim is being decided, and why the difference matters |
| Evidence | Source IDs, exact read locations, actual findings, access/read limits and evidence versions |
| Alternatives | Plausible treatments and consequences, including retaining distinct variants or leaving a question open when useful |
| Selection and reason | Chosen adaptation or factual treatment; basis in the project's goals and available evidence; status as established choice or working assumption |
| Attribution | What is source-derived, interpretive, combined from identified variants, or newly invented; any reader-facing explanation required by the project |
| Residual uncertainty | What remains unknown, conflicting or unsupported; resolution milestone and blocked commitment if any |
| Application and verification | Decision ID to actual text, supplements, names, art and other affected destinations, with versioned review evidence |

Do not average incompatible accounts into a claim of consensus. A combination may be a valid fictional choice when authorized by the adaptation scope, but label it as a combination. Invented dialogue, powers, relationships or rituals do not become documented tradition because a traditional figure appears in the scene. Conversely, do not erase a documented variant by describing it as your invention. Keep quotations, translations, paraphrases and new prose distinguishable.

When uncertainty is material, use precise bounded language in factual reader-facing material, or omit an unsupported claim where that serves the brief. Decide whether an author's note is useful from the requested reader package; do not require a disclaimer in every fictional scene. Public availability alone does not establish reuse rights. Check rights only as needed for the material and intended use; record an unresolved dependency without presenting a creative-source choice as legal clearance.

## Synthetic example

Suppose two supplied excerpts place the same fictional meeting before and after a journey. The excerpts support a chronology difference; they do not establish why the editions differ or which reflects a broader tradition. Record their actual edition information and reading limits. Alternatives might preserve edition A's order, adopt edition B's order, or create a disclosed new sequence. Select the treatment that serves the agreed adaptation, then trace it to the relevant outline, chapter, recap and scene art. If the rest of either edition is unread, do not claim full fidelity or that no other contradictions exist.

## Reopen only affected work

When a new passage, translation, correction or user choice changes a decision, retain the old decision as superseded. Follow its destination links and inspect likely omissions: narrative, recaps, profiles, afterwords, citations, maps, art, metadata and covers where relevant. Recheck character/reader knowledge if the change moves a reveal. Record changed, unchanged, pending and unverified destinations separately.

Use [continuity and knowledge](continuity-and-knowledge.md) for resulting canon and reveal changes, and [release and recovery](release-and-recovery.md) for exact-version handoffs. Do not rewrite the history of what was actually consulted or treat a new selected source as a fresh reading of every prior one.
